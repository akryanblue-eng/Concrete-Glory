# Concrete Glory

**Three-minute street roller hockey. Polyurethane and pavement.**

Concrete Glory is a fast, arcade-flavored 3-on-3 street roller hockey game built
for mobile. Matches are short, loud, and physical: you skate a chain-link arena,
bank pucks off the fence, dodge readable concrete hazards, and settle grudges
with the occasional asphalt brawl. It is inspired by the feel of classic arcade
hockey — the snap of a one-timer, the crowd-pop of a big check — but every crew,
arena, archetype, and mechanic is original to Concrete Glory.

---

## The Pitch

- **Format:** 3-on-3, street roller hockey on concrete.
- **Length:** three minutes of play — three 60-second periods — so a full match
  fits in a bus stop, a coffee line, or a commercial break.
- **Feel:** arcade-first. Momentum, rebounds, and reads matter more than sim
  accuracy. Easy to pick up, hard to master.
- **Tone:** 90s-neon, chiptune-loud, chrome-and-graffiti street culture.

## Core Loop

One touch does one thing. The whole game is built from five verbs:

| Verb      | Input            | What it does                                        |
| --------- | ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Skate** | Drag             | Steer your active skater; momentum carries.         |
| **Pass**  | Tap a teammate   | Snap the puck along the ground to a crew member.    |
| **Shoot** | Flick            | Flick toward the net; flick speed sets shot power.  |
| **Check** | Drag into rival  | Body a puck-carrier to knock the puck loose.        |
| **Rebound** | (positional)   | Read the fence-bank and crash the loose puck.       |

Everything else — brawls, hazards, the Heat system — layers on top of this
movement/pass/shoot/check/rebound spine.

## Signature Systems

### Fence-Bank Physics
The arena is a cage. Walls are live surfaces: pucks carom off the chain-link at
predictable angles, so banking a pass or a shot off the fence is a real,
learnable play — not a lucky bounce.

### Readable Concrete Hazards
Cracks, drains, oil slicks, and curb lips live on the play surface. Every hazard
is telegraphed and consistent: hit one and you know why. Hazards reward map
knowledge, never punish blind.

### The Asphalt Brawl
Tempers run hot. When collisions stack up, play breaks into a short, contained
brawl — a rhythm-tap duel with its own health pools. Win it and you swing
momentum; lose it and you eat a penalty. Brawls are a spice, not the meal.

### Heat & Rivalries
Crews build **Heat** against each other across matches. Rivalry games raise the
stakes: hotter brawls, louder crowds, better rewards. Your history with an
opponent is part of the fiction.

### Crews, Arenas, and Archetypes
- **Crews** — original street teams, each with a distinct silhouette and
  attitude.
- **Arenas** — original concrete courts, each with its own fence geometry and
  hazard layout.
- **Archetypes** — player roles tuned around the three core stats:
  **Speed** (wheels), **Handling** (bearings), and **Power** (shot velocity).

## Monetization

Premium up front **or** a one-time unlock. No pay-to-win. Gear changes how you
play — feel, not raw advantage — and nothing that affects the outcome of a match
is sold. No energy timers, no lootbox sludge.

## Milestone: CG-001 — First Playable Slice

The first vertical slice proves the core is fun before anything else is built.

**Scope of CG-001:**

- **One arena** — a single concrete court with its fence geometry and hazards.
- **One 60-second period** — a single period of live play, not a full match.
- **The core loop, end to end** — movement, pass, shoot, check, and rebound all
  working together against a basic opponent.

**Out of scope for CG-001:** meta-progression, the Heat/rivalry system, gear
economy, multiple arenas, full three-period matches, and online play. Those are
built on top of the slice once the loop feels right.

The bar for CG-001 is simple: a stranger can pick it up, skate, pass, shoot,
check, chase a rebound, and want to run it back — inside one 60-second period.

---

## Originality Boundary

Concrete Glory is **inspired by arcade hockey, not a clone of any existing
title.** It is not *Blades of Steel* or any other specific game, and it must not
reproduce another game's names, characters, logos, teams, audio, art, level
layouts, or copyrighted text.

What we take from the genre is *feel* — short matches, big hits, snappy
controls, arcade energy. What we make ourselves is *everything nameable*: the
crews, arenas, archetypes, hazards, the Heat system, the brawl mechanic, the
art, and the audio. When in doubt, invent it.
